# Perception Principles

The philosophical bridge between cognitive intelligence engineering and industrial embodiment design.

---

## 1. Why Luca Feels Calm
- **Grounded Stability**: Motion never jumps, steps, or jitters. Inertia smooths every transition so presence feels serene and unhurried.
- **Visual Quietness**: Luminance and opacity remain softly balanced. Luca never produces bright flashes or distracting high-frequency noise.

---

## 2. Non-Aggressive Presence
- **No Demanding Attention**: Luca sits comfortably in peripheral vision. It waits to be addressed rather than shouting for attention.
- **Gentle Boundaries**: Edge reflectivity and bloom fade smoothly into dark backgrounds without harsh outlines or neon halos.

---

## 3. Expressing Confidence
- **Understated Composure**: Confidence is expressed through steady, slow breathing and a deep volumetric core glow—never through increased size or bright colors.
- **Subtle Alignment**: In idle states, the orb maintains a balanced, upright liquid posture with subtle teardrop bottom sag.

---

## 4. Expressing Uncertainty & Thinking
- **Internal Focusing**: When processing, luminosity shifts inward toward the core while the outer glass surface becomes slightly cooler and more crystalline.
- **No Confused Wobble**: Uncertainty is communicated through internal light dynamics, never through erratic movement or cartoonish squash-and-stretch.

---

## 5. Excluded Emotional Range (Strictly Forbidden)
- ❌ **Aggression / Panic**: Flashy red strobing, rapid pulsing, or erratic vibration.
- ❌ **Performative Excitement**: Cartoonish bouncing, particle explosions, or rainbow color cycles.
- ❌ **Mechanical Servitude**: Robotic blinking, artificial progress bars, or rigid geometric transformations.

---

## 6. Multi-Embodiment Compliance
All presentational embodiments (`Living Orb`, `Hologram Face`, `Minimal Dot`, `AR Projection`, `Robotics`) must adhere to these Perception Principles to ensure Luca's cognitive identity remains consistent regardless of the visual surface.
